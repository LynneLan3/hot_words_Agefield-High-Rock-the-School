Agefield is a small, handcrafted open world. The useful question is not “how many square kilometers,” but **which area you should be in for the current part of the day**.

- **Campus** is for the school day.
- **Neighborhoods** are for residential streets, roaming, and some cash jobs.
- **Town center** is for shops and town activities.
- **Countryside** is for wider travel and exploration.

How those areas fit a weekday is on the [Agefield High Gameplay](/agefield-high-rock-the-school/gameplay/) loop.

Steam and the official “Who Should Attend Agefield High?” announcement describe a smaller world with personality — not a massive AAA map. This page does not invent a size.

## School / Campus

**What it’s for:** weekday school. This is the home of the Time & School System — classrooms, the timetable, and the start of each school day.

**When you go:** weekday mornings through afternoon, whenever you intend to attend class. Near class time, the in-game map helps you find the room.

**How it connects:** attending class here is the pocket-money path through grades. Skipping class usually means leaving campus for jobs or exploration, with teachers and cops as the stated risk. After class, the useful next step is the after-school story, not hanging around campus with no plan.

Interior layout, room names, and a floor plan are <span class="unconfirmed">not officially mapped yet</span>. Lesson types and verified class notes are on the [Agefield High Classes & School System](/agefield-high-rock-the-school/classes/) guide.

## Neighborhood / Residential area

**What it’s for:** the residential side of Agefield. Official Steam copy includes **two neighborhoods** in the open world. Developer making-of posts also show the town being built out over time. Official names for those two neighborhoods are <span class="unconfirmed">not published</span>, so this guide does not invent district names.

**When you go:** after school, or during class hours if you skip. This is the residential side of exploration once you leave campus.

**How it connects:** missions and free-roam both send you off campus into these streets. Official cash activities include **lawn mowing**; this page does not pin that job, or any other side job, to a named street.

## Town Center

**What it’s for:** the small-town sandbox — shops and town activities. Official press mentions place types such as **bike shops**, **tattoo parlors**, **corner stores**, and **arcades**.

**When you go:** when you have cash to spend, or when you need a bike, clothes, or another listed shop item.

**How it connects:** this is the spending end of the money loop. Grades and side jobs earn cash; town-center shops are where that cash gets used. Official materials also list **food delivery** as a cash job, but this page does not pin delivery routes to the town center. Exact storefront coordinates are <span class="unconfirmed">not verified</span>.

## Countryside

**What it’s for:** the open ground beyond town — wider travel and exploration rather than classrooms or shop streets.

**When you go:** when a mission or exploration takes you off the town grid, or when you want space to ride.

**How it connects:** the official Steam announcement mentions **woods behind the school** in the context of country bikes for off-roading. Trail maps and countryside POIs are <span class="unconfirmed">not officially listed</span>. Watch for collectibles here the same way you would in town — without expecting a pin list on this page.

## Bikes and transportation

A bike is the practical way to move between campus, neighborhoods, town center, and countryside.

Official shop lists include bikes. Steam names **BMX**, **country**, and **racing** bikes as types you can buy or use. Country bikes are called out for off-roading. Prices, stats, unlocks, and whether Sam starts with a bike are <span class="unconfirmed">not independently verified</span>.

You do not need a separate transportation guide. Get comfortable with the four area types, then use a bike to cut travel time during the after-school window. That loop is covered in [Agefield High Gameplay](/agefield-high-rock-the-school/gameplay/).

## Shops

Confirmed things cash can buy:

- Bikes
- Clothes
- Hairstyles
- Tattoos
- Accessories

Press copy also refers to bike shops and tattoo parlors as place types. Steam mentions other shop items.

This page does not place those shops on a map. Prices and NPC positions are <span class="unconfirmed">not verified</span>. For earning methods, see [How to Make Money in Agefield High](/agefield-high-rock-the-school/how-to-make-money/).

## Mission-related locations

Main missions follow the **Rock the School** plan with Kale and Axel. Official materials place that story in the same small world as exploration — campus, neighborhoods, town, and countryside — not in a separate mission hub.

After class, check the current story beat before you spend the whole afternoon on side jobs. This page does **not** publish a mission list, a walkthrough, or a pin for each objective. Those still need independently verified player evidence.

## Collectibles

The official Steam announcement says that outside classes and story missions you will find **collectibles and secrets**. That confirms they exist.

While you explore campus, neighborhoods, town center, and countryside, keep an eye out. Do not wait for a completion checklist from this site.

## Agefield High Map FAQ

### What is the school campus for?

Weekday school. Go there for classes, the timetable, and the start of each school day. After class, you usually leave campus for missions, jobs, and town.

### Where do you shop and make money?

Town center is the main place type for shops. Neighborhoods are the residential streets you explore once you leave campus. Exact storefront pins and job spawns are not mapped here.

### How do you get around?

By bike, between campus, neighborhoods, town center, and countryside. Official lists include BMX, country, and racing bikes.

### Are there collectibles?

Yes. Watch for them while exploring the main areas. Counts, exact locations, and 100% requirements are not verified on this page.

### How big is the map?

Officially described as a small handcrafted indie world. No km² figure has been published.

### Are there named neighborhoods?

Two neighborhoods are confirmed. Official names are <span class="unconfirmed">not published</span>.
